# Funk song v34

v33 with the master EQ checked against published data: Pestana, Ma, Reiss et al., "Spectral Characteristics of Popular Commercial Recordings 1950-2010", AES 135th Convention, 2013.

- **Reference:** commercial mixes fall about 5 dB per octave from 100 Hz to 4 kHz, getting steeper above. The paper's Table 2 gives log-log spectral slopes of -1.03 for soul and R&B, -0.80 for disco, and about -0.94 to -0.98 for pop and rock.
- **v33:** the slope was -0.87 overall. From 2 to 8 kHz the spectrum rose (+0.9 dB per octave) where records keep falling, which is the harsh top end you heard from the new cymbals.
- **v34:** the old +1.5 dB treble boost is gone. Instead there's a high shelf of -3.5 dB from 4.5 kHz and a 1.5 dB presence lift around 2 kHz. The overall slope is now -1.10, between soul/R&B and a little darker. 2 to 8 kHz now falls about 2.4 dB per octave, and 100 Hz to 4 kHz is unchanged at -4.4.

Octave bands relative to 1 kHz, v33 vs v34: 4 kHz -2.5 vs -4.3, 8 kHz -1.4 vs -6.0, 16 kHz -7.3 vs -12.6.
