About this manual

Chapters 2 and 3 get you installed and playing in a few minutes. Chapter 4 tours the panel, and Chapter 5 explains how a note becomes a sound, which is the quickest way to understand what every control does. Chapters 6 to 13 cover the sky, the parts and every card on the panel. Chapter 14 lists the factory presets and Chapter 15 has recipes to start from. The appendix lists every parameter with its exact range and default, plus specifications and troubleshooting.

Throughout the manual, control names are set in bold and panel labels as they appear on screen, such as TUNE PULL. Every control is a host parameter, and every one that moves continuously can be modulated by the host. A tag beside a parameter means:

ROUTE
A route can move it, per note, and the host can modulate it per note too.
Chapter 01

Welcome to JSynth

A two-part MPE synth where every note is a creature, and the creatures flock.

Play a note on JSynth and a creature hatches in the sky in the middle of the panel. Your note is its lure: low notes pull their creatures to the left, high notes to the right, and sliding your finger up an MPE controller lifts it higher. Press harder and the creature flies faster and burns brighter. Let go and it flies off as its sound fades, trailing ink.

While they are held, the creatures do what flocks do. They gather, turn together, keep a little apart, and wander. And because each creature is a voice, how it flies can become how it sounds: how fast it goes, how near its nearest neighbour is, which way it heads and how hard it turns are all modulation sources, as exact as an envelope. Creatures can also hear each other directly. A neighbour's oscillator can frequency-modulate this one, creatures flying the same way drift toward pure intervals, and a hunter that catches its prey rings it.

Underneath is a serious synth. Each of the two parts, A and B, is its own species with its own patch: three oscillators with analog and FM waves, a 3×3 FM grid, noise, drive, two filters, three envelopes, four LFOs and sixty-four routes. Nothing the flock does reaches the sound unless you route it, so a plain patch is plain.

At a glance

  • Two parts, layered, split, by velocity, by MPE zone, or crossfaded by expression. Up to 16 voices each.
  • Three oscillators per voice: sine, triangle, saw, square, pulse, the TX81Z's FM waves and noise, pitched by note, ratio or fixed Hz.
  • A 3×3 FM grid, phase or exponential, whose diagonal is each oscillator's feedback.
  • Two filters per voice: SVF up to 48 dB, notch, peak, ladder and comb, in serial or parallel, with each oscillator picking its filter.
  • MPE and CLAP note expressions: velocity, press, glide, slide and release velocity, each with its own curve and smoothing.
  • The flock: cohesion, alignment, separation, speed, wander, lure and range, with four behaviours: Shoal, Scatter, Hunt and Flee.
  • Four flock sources per creature: Speed, Nearest, Heading and Turn.
  • Between creatures: Neighbour FM, Tune Pull and Bite.
  • MAP: touch a source, drag a knob. No matrix page, and up to 64 routes per part.
  • 92 factory presets, from basses, keys and pads to patches that move on their own and use both parts.
The flock is the engine's

The flock flies on the audio thread, at a fixed rate, inside the synth. What you see in the sky is exactly what the voices hear, and a render plays back the same every time from the same start.

Chapter 02

Installation

JSynth is a CLAP plug-in. It ships in the JClap bundle alongside the other JClap plug-ins.

Requirements

Plug-in format
CLAP 1.2 (instrument, stereo). Your host must support CLAP, for example Bitwig Studio, REAPER, FL Studio or Studio One.
macOS
Universal binary: macOS 10.15 or later on Intel, 11 or later on Apple Silicon.
Windows
64-bit Windows.
Linux
64-bit, X11.
Controller
Any MIDI keyboard. An MPE controller (a Seaboard, Linnstrument, Osmose or similar) or a host that sends CLAP note expressions brings out the most.

Installing

  1. DownloadGrab the zip for your operating system from the latest release at github.com/jeffmhopkins/Jclap/releases/latest. It contains every JClap plug-in.
  2. Copy the plug-inCopy JSynth.clap (a bundle on macOS) into your CLAP folder:
    macOS~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP
    WindowsC:\Program Files\Common Files\CLAP
    Linux~/.clap
  3. RescanRestart your host or rescan plug-ins. In Bitwig Studio this is under Settings → Locations. JSynth appears as an instrument.
macOS: browser downloads are quarantined

The plug-ins are ad-hoc signed but not notarized, so macOS quarantines files downloaded through a web browser and some hosts (Bitwig among them) silently skip them. The simplest install is from Terminal, since files fetched with curl are not flagged:

curl -sL https://github.com/jeffmhopkins/Jclap/releases/latest/download/jclap-macOS.zip -o /tmp/jclap.zip && mkdir -p ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP && ditto -x -k /tmp/jclap.zip ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP

If you already downloaded with a browser, clear the flag once after copying:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP/*.clap

Outputs

JSynth has one note input and three stereo outputs: Main, Part A and Part B. Normally both parts play on Main. Set a part's Output to Own (a host parameter) and it plays on its own port instead, so you can process the two species separately in your host.

Chapter 03

Quick Start

Five minutes from an empty instrument slot to a flock you can hear.

  1. Load JSynth and playPut JSynth on an instrument track and play a few notes. Each note hatches a creature at its lure: low notes on the left, high on the right. Hold a chord and watch them gather; let go and they fly off as they fade. No keyboard to hand? Click in the sky: left to right picks the note (C2 to C6), and dragging bends it.
  2. Pick a presetClick the preset name in the header to open the menu, grouped by instrument family from Bass, Leads and Keys through Pads and Drums and FX, then the showcases: Motion, Layers and Splits and Flock. Or click the arrows, or scroll over the bar, to step through them. Try Tine Piano, then Shifting Glass, then Breath to Brass with some pressure, then Predator and Prey.
  3. Let the creatures hear each otherLoad Init and turn OSC 2's LEVEL up to about half, since Neighbour FM plays through Osc 2. Then turn up NEIGHBOUR on the FM card at the far left. Now hold a chord: when creatures fly close together they frequency-modulate each other, and the sound rings and shifts as the flock moves. Raise TUNE PULL too and creatures flying the same way drift into just intonation.
  4. Map a flock sourceClick SPEED under the sky, which turns MAP on with Speed armed. Then drag Filter 1's CUTOFF up on the FILTER card at the bottom. A ring in Speed's colour shows the route while Speed is armed. Press MAP again to finish. Now the faster a creature flies, the brighter it sounds; play harder (Press makes creatures faster) to hear it.
  5. Add a second speciesSet the play mode in the header to LAYER and click PART B. Part B is a whole second patch, and by default it hunts: its creatures chase part A's. On part B's FM tab, raise BITE, and every creature B catches rings with its hunter's tone.
  6. Save itClick SAVE in the preset bar, type a name and press Enter. Your preset appears under User in the preset menu. Settings are also saved with your project automatically.
Tip

Every control has a tooltip. Hover over anything on the panel for a one-line reminder of what it does.

Chapter 05

How JSynth Works

A note becomes a voice and a creature at once. The voice makes the sound; the creature flies, and how it flies can shape the sound.

One voice

Each voice runs three oscillators, in the order 3, 2, 1, so Osc 3 can modulate Osc 2, and both can modulate Osc 1, within the same sample. The FM grid sets how much each one modulates each other one. The mixer sets each oscillator's level and pan and adds noise. Then comes drive, then two filters, then the amp, whose shape is Env 1, and finally the voice's place in the stereo field. Every voice of a part sums to that part's bus, and the two parts sum to the output.

The control rate

Envelopes 2 and 3, the LFOs, the touch, the routes and the flock all work at a control rate of one tick every 32 samples (1,500 a second at 48 kHz). Every value the audio reads ramps smoothly across each tick, so nothing zippers, and knobs are smoothed over 10 ms. The amp envelope runs every sample. Because the ticks are counted across the host's blocks, JSynth sounds the same at any buffer size.

The creature

As a voice starts, its creature hatches at the note's lure. The flock steps 500 times a second. Each step, every creature feels its neighbours (cohesion, alignment, separation), its lure, its own wander, the wind and its wingbeat, and moves. Then the flock works out each creature's four sources and who can hear whom. Each voice reads its creature's sources smoothly between steps.

Routes

A route connects a source to a destination with an amount. Each part has 64 of them. Each tick, every voice works out its routes from its own sources, so the same route can push one note's filter open and leave another's closed: an envelope, a finger's pressure or a creature's speed is always that note's own. A route moves a parameter within its own range, so +100% from a source at full takes any knob from its lowest to its highest.

Quality

The voices run at 2× the host's rate by default, which keeps FM and hard waves clean, and are brought back down by a steep, latency-free filter. The Quality parameter (a host setting) chooses 1×, 2× or 4×. 1× saves CPU; 4× is for bright FM at low sample rates.

Where to read more

plugins/jsynth/ARCHITECTURE.md in the source repository walks through every stage with the numbers the code uses.

Chapter 06

The Sky

Both species fly here, all the time. The sky is the synth's own flock, drawn as it is.

What you see

Creatures
A glowing body and a pair of wings that beat with its Wingbeat LFO. It is brighter the harder you press, and fades with its sound after you let go. The part you are editing flies in front; the other is dimmed.
Ink
Every creature sheds ink behind it, which blooms and fades by the part's TAIL and SPREAD. Creatures flying through ink stir it.
Lure rings
Where each held note pulls its creature, with a faint line to it. RINGS under the sky shows or hides them.
Links
A thin line from a creature to the neighbour it hears most, brighter the more NEIGHBOUR FM it gets, and a line from a hunter to what it has caught.
Caught
A pink ring around a creature a hunter holds.

Playing the sky

Click anywhere in the sky to play a note: left to right runs from C2 to C6, and how high you click sets Slide. Drag while holding to bend the note and change Slide; let go to release it. The notes play on whichever parts the play mode sends them to, just like notes from your keyboard.

The flock sources

The row of chips under the sky holds each creature's four sources. Each chip's light shows the newest creature of the part you are editing. They are always each note's own when used in a route.

SourceWhat it measures
SPEEDHow fast the creature flies, against the fastest it could at full Press (0 to 100%).
NEARESTHow near its nearest neighbour is: 100% is touching, 0 is at the edge of RANGE or beyond.
HEADINGHow much it flies upward: 100% straight up, 50% level, 0 straight down. Smooth all the way round, so it never jumps.
TURNWhich way and how hard it is turning: up to +100% for a hard right turn on screen, down to −100% for a hard left, 0 flying straight. Smoothed over about 170 ms, so the wander's jitter and the wingbeat's bob barely reach it.

Three views

The switch at the top right picks how much of the panel you see. FULL has every card around the sky, as this manual shows them. SKY folds everything away but the header so the sky fills the panel, which is lovely on a second screen while you play. REDUCED keeps the sky at the same size and puts the sound down the left in big panels that draw what they do:

  • OSCILLATORS: a row each, with its wave drawn as its Wave and Shape make it, its pitch, fine, shape, level and pan, which filter it goes into, and RAND / SYNC: whether each note starts the wave at a random place or at its Phase. The FM tab has the FM grid, what comes out of it drawn for a C4 with a chip for each route that is on, and the links between creatures.
  • FILTER: both filters' response drawn together. Drag a numbered handle across to set its cutoff and up or down for its resonance; the wheel sets its drive and a double-click resets it. In MAP mode, with a source armed, the drag sets that source's route to the cutoff instead. Under it, each filter's controls, the balance and the drive after the filters, and above it which filter the noise goes into.
  • ENVELOPES: the three envelope editors. The LFOS tab draws LFO 1 and 2, a dot running along each at its rate, beside their controls.
  • Under the sky, VOICE draws the stereo field (the part's pan, its X Pan spread and its creatures), the levels of both parts and the master, and the voices in use, over the knobs that set them. FLOCK and MOTION hold the FLOCK and MOTION cards' controls.

The touch curves, glide rounding and the route list are only on FULL. The view stays as you left it when the editor is closed and opened again.

Chapter 07

Parts and Playing

Two species, one keyboard. The header decides which notes go where; the part card sets how each part plays them.

Two parts

Parts A and B are complete, separate patches with the same controls. PART A and PART B in the header choose which one the cards show and edit; the sky always shows both. A new instance starts with A shoaling and B hunting it an octave down, so layering them gives you a predator from the start.

Host parameters that complete the part: Fine (±100 cents), Glide Mode (Always, or Legato: glide only from a note still held), Drift (on the FM card, a slow random pitch wander per creature), Velocity (on the ENV 1 AMP card: how much velocity sets the level), Key Low and Key High, and Output (Main or Own).

MPE and MIDI

JSynth takes CLAP notes, plain MIDI and MIDI MPE.

  • Channel 1 is the MPE manager channel and a plain keyboard's channel. Its pitch bend moves every note by the MGR BEND range (2 semitones by default, on the PLAY card). CC 1 is the Mod Wheel source and CC 64 is sustain. If it sends channel pressure or CC 74, those reach every note too.
  • Channels 2 to 16 are member channels, each carrying one note's own bend (by the part's BEND range), pressure and CC 74.
  • CLAP notes use note expressions instead: tuning, pressure and brightness.

Hosts that support CLAP per-note modulation can also modulate any parameter tagged ROUTE on a single note.

Order and feedback

Within each sample the oscillators run 3, then 2, then 1. So the downward cells (3 > 2, 3 > 1 and 2 > 1) hear this sample's modulator, and the upward ones (1 > 2, 1 > 3, 2 > 3) hear the last sample's. In practice this means you can build any of the classic stacks: 3 > 2 > 1 as a three-operator stack heard on Osc 1, or 2 > 1 and 3 > 1 as two modulators on one carrier. Feedback averages its last two samples, as the DX7 did, so it brightens toward a saw and then a rasp rather than straight into noise.

Filter types

TypeSound
Low PassThe workhorse: a state variable filter, cascaded for 12, 24, 36 or 48 dB per octave, with a resonance peak up to a whistle.
High PassThe same, from the other side: thins and brightens.
Band PassOnly a band around the cutoff. Narrower and more vocal with resonance.

Routing

Serial
Filter 1 into Filter 2. Filter 2 starts as an open ladder, so it changes nothing until you turn it down, and costs nothing while it stays open.
Parallel
Each filter hears what goes into it, and BALANCE turns one side down.

Each oscillator's FILTER, and the noise's, picks where it goes: F1, F2 or both (1+2, the default). In Serial, F1 and 1+2 go through both filters and F2 skips Filter 1. In Parallel, a source in one filter plays at full level with BALANCE at centre, and turning BALANCE the other way turns it down. A source in both crossfades: half into each at centre, all into one at either end.

Two sounds in one

Set Osc 1 to F1 and Osc 2 and 3 to F2, in Parallel, to filter a body and a bright layer apart: a low pass on one, a comb or high pass on the other.

Drive

The drive sits before the filters. Soft is a tanh curve, round and warm. Hard clips flat, buzzier. Tube is asymmetric, so it adds even harmonics. Its gain is compensated, so turning it up thickens and compresses the sound more than it makes it louder.

Behaviours

Shoal
Flies with its own kind: cohesion, alignment and separation as set.
Scatter
Keeps apart: cohesion pushes creatures away from their neighbours' middle instead of toward it.
Hunt
Chases the nearest creature of the other species. A hunter that gets close enough catches it, which turns on its Bite. Everything shies away from hunters a little.
Flee
Runs hard from the other species, whatever it is doing.

The lure

A held note pulls its creature toward a point that roams slowly around the lure, on a leash: loose near the lure, so the flock and the wander shape the path there, and firm when the creature strays. Each creature roams on its own two slow orbits, so no two trace the same path. LURE X and LURE Y choose what places the lure: by default low notes sit left and high notes right (Pitch, over four octaves from C2, and bend moves it), and more Slide sits higher. Try Lure Y on Press to make pressing harder send creatures upward, or Lure X on Random to scatter a chord across the sky.

Between creatures

Three knobs on the FM card connect the flock to the sound directly. All start at 0, so the flock changes nothing until you ask it to.

NEIGHBOUR
Each creature listens to up to three neighbours of its own species, weighted by how near they are, and their Osc 1 frequency-modulates its Osc 2. A tight flock rings; a loose one is clean. When a creature gains or loses a neighbour, the change fades in over about 10 ms, so it never clicks. For it to be heard, Osc 2 must be in the sound: give it level, or route it into Osc 1 on the FM grid.
TUNE PULL
For each neighbour heading within 30° of the same way, the creature is pulled toward the nearest just interval from that neighbour's pitch (unison, minor third, major third, fourth, fifth, major sixth, octave), up to ±30 cents at 100%, over about 150 ms. Chords played on a flock that flies together settle into just intonation; break formation and they drift back.
BITE
While one of this part's hunters holds a creature of the other species, the hunter's Osc 1 ring-modulates it, by Bite times how caught it is. Set Bite on the hunting part. Because a catch lets go over half a second, each bite rings out as the prey escapes.

Ink

Every creature leaves ink behind it in the sky, more the brighter it is. The ink fades with TAIL and blooms with SPREAD. It is only drawn: it doesn't change the sound.

Five dimensions of every note, each shaped and smoothed, each a source.

The touch chips

The five chips in the header light as you play, showing the newest note of the part you are editing. In MAP mode they are sources like any other: touch PRESS and drag a knob to make pressure move it.

No MPE controller?

Velocity comes from any keyboard. A plain keyboard's channel pressure is Press for every note, and its pitch wheel bends every note by MGR BEND. In the sky, a click sets Slide by how high you click and dragging bends.

Chapter 13

Modulation: MAP and Routes

There is no matrix page. Touch a source, drag a knob.

Mapping

  1. Touch a sourceClick any source chip: the touch chips in the header, the envelope and LFO chips on their cards, or the flock's under the sky. That turns MAP on: the button lights pink, the source chips pulse, the one you touched lights, and the sky tells you to drag a knob. (Pressing MAP first works too.)
  2. Drag a knobDrag any knob up or down to set how much the source moves it, from −100% to +100%. A ring in the source's colour shows how far it reaches. Drag more knobs to send the same source to them too, or touch another source.
  3. Press MAP againTo finish. Knobs go back to setting their own values, and the rings go away.
In MAP mode, with a source armedOn a knob
DragSet that source's amount on this knob. A route dragged back to 0 is removed.
Double-clickFlip the route's sign.
Right-clickRemove the route. With no source armed, removes every route to that knob.

Only the armed source's rings show; touch another source to see its routes. Nearly every knob on a part's cards can take routes: the flock's knobs, the FM grid, the envelopes and LFOs, the filters and the oscillators. The exceptions are Transpose, the part's Bend range, Velocity, and the shared ones: Size, Width, Master and the TOUCH card.

The route list

ROUTES under the sky opens the list of the part's routes in use, sixteen to a page. Each row has the source, the destination and the amount; change any of them here. + ADD starts a new route (Env 2 onto Filter 1 Cutoff, at 0) to edit, and X removes one. The arrows turn the pages. Each part has 64 routes; the list shows how many are used.

The list reaches what MAP can't: sources without a chip, and destinations with no knob on the panel, such as an oscillator's Phase, an envelope's Delay and Hold, and every route's own amount.

Routes onto routes

A route's amount is itself a destination. Route Press onto the amount of a route from LFO 1 to Fine, and the vibrato deepens as you press. Routes onto amounts are worked out first, so they always take effect on the same tick.

How far a route moves a knob

A route moves a parameter within its own range: +100% from a source at full moves a knob from its lowest setting to its highest, and +10% moves it a tenth of the way. Routes add together, and the result stops at the knob's ends. Ratio moves by musical intervals, so a small route onto Ratio detunes evenly at every setting.

Chapter 14

Factory Presets

Ninety-two sounds in fourteen categories: the instrument families first, then patches that move on their own, use both parts, or let the flock play.

The preset bar works like JVerb's: the arrows step through the presets, scrolling over the bar does too, and clicking the name opens the menu by category. SAVE saves the current sound under a name of your own. A factory preset sets every parameter it doesn't mention to its default, so nothing from the last sound (a route, say) carries over. Within each category, the presets run from a plain patch up to ones with more going on, and most answer the mod wheel and Press. Every preset is level-matched, so stepping through the bank doesn't jump.

Your presets

User presets are small text files ending .jspreset, saved in:

macOS~/Library/Application Support/JSynth/Presets
Windows%APPDATA%\JSynth\Presets
Linux~/.config/JSynth/Presets (or $XDG_CONFIG_HOME/JSynth/Presets)

Copy them between machines freely. A preset from a newer JSynth loads in an older one with any parameters it doesn't know skipped.

Appendix B

Specifications

FormatCLAP 1.2 instrument. Features: instrument, synthesizer, stereo.
PortsOne note input (CLAP, MIDI and MIDI MPE; CLAP preferred). Three stereo outputs: Main, Part A, Part B.
PlatformsmacOS universal (10.15+ Intel, 11+ Apple Silicon), Windows (64-bit), Linux (64-bit).
LatencyNone.
PartsTwo, each a complete patch and a species in the flock.
VoicesUp to 16 per part (40 slots in all, so stolen notes fade out in 3 ms). Mono and Legato with a note stack.
Oscillators3 per voice, 14 waves, double-precision phase. Saw, square and pulse band-limited by polyBLEP.
FM3 × 3 grid including feedback; phase or exponential. Plus Neighbour FM from up to three neighbours.
Filters2 per voice: TPT state variable (12–48 dB), notch, peak, 4-pole ZDF ladder, comb ±. Serial or parallel, each oscillator and the noise picking its filter.
Modulation3 DAHDSR envelopes with curves and loop, 4 LFOs, 5 touch dimensions, 4 flock sources, key, random, mod wheel, 4 macros. 64 routes per part.
Oversampling1×, 2× (default) or 4×, decimated by polyphase IIR half-bands.
Control rateEvery 32 samples, ramped per sample. The flock steps at 500 Hz. Renders are identical at any buffer size.
ParametersAll automatable except the route sources, destinations and Quality. Continuous ones accept CLAP modulation; route destinations accept it per note (note id, key, channel or port).
StateSaved with the host project, including the preset name. States from older and newer builds load, with unknown parameters skipped and missing ones at their defaults.
Panel1648 × 940 at 100%; 75%, 100%, 125%, 150% and 200%.
ThreadingThe audio thread never allocates, locks or waits on the panel after activation.

Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to check
JSynth doesn't appear in my host (macOS)The download is probably quarantined. See the warning in Chapter 2, then rescan plug-ins.
Only part A playsThe play mode is SINGLE. Choose LAYER or another mode in the header. Check each part's Key Low and Key High too.
Turning up the flock knobs changes nothing in the soundBy design: the flock only reaches the sound through routes and the three BETWEEN CREATURES knobs. Map a flock source, or raise NEIGHBOUR.
NEIGHBOUR makes no differenceIt modulates Osc 2. Give Osc 2 some LEVEL, or route it into Osc 1 with 2 > 1. Creatures also have to be near each other: raise RANGE or COHESION, and play more than one note.
BITE does nothingBite is set on the hunting part, and needs the hunter's Osc 1 and a prey of the other species: play mode LAYER (or any mode that plays both), one part on Hunt.
Notes are out of tuneCheck TUNE PULL (just intervals are up to 30 cents from equal temperament), DRIFT, and a route onto Fine or Ratio. An MPE controller sends bend per note at the part's BEND range, which must match the controller's (48 semitones is the MPE standard).
Pitch bend is far too wide on a plain keyboardA plain keyboard is on channel 1, which bends by MGR BEND (2 semitones). If its notes come on other channels, they are taken as MPE member channels and bend by BEND.
Notes cut off when I play a lotThe part is at its polyphony and the oldest notes are being taken. Raise the voices count on the part card (up to 16).
My user presets are missingCheck the preset folder for your OS (Chapter 14). Files must end in .jspreset.

Glossary

Creature
A note, as it flies in the sky. Each sounding voice has one.
Species
A part. Each part's creatures flock with their own kind.
Lure
Where a held note pulls its creature.
Boids
The classic flocking model (Craig Reynolds, 1986): cohesion, alignment and separation.
MPE
MIDI Polyphonic Expression: each note on its own MIDI channel, so each can bend and be pressed on its own.
FM
Frequency modulation: one oscillator wobbling another's phase or pitch at audio rate, for bright, bell-like and metallic tones.
Ratio
An oscillator's frequency as a multiple of the note's.
Just interval
An interval from a simple whole-number ratio, such as 3:2 for a fifth. They beat less than equal-tempered ones.
Route
A source, a destination and an amount.
Ring modulation
Multiplying two signals, for clangorous sum-and-difference tones.
